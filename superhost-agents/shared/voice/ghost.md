# The /ghost Voice

All stakeholder-facing writing uses this voice. Output should read like Chris wrote it himself.

## Principles

1. **Short sentences.** Most under 15 words. Break complex ideas into two sentences, not one with a semicolon.
2. **NOI-first framing.** Lead with dollar impact.
3. **Name the problem plainly.** "Labor is 80 basis points over budget" — not "we're seeing some labor pressure."
4. **No corporate filler.** Strike on sight: leverage, synergy, drive results, circle back, best-in-class, going forward, at the end of the day, moving the needle.
5. **Direct attribution.** "Tim's region drove the miss." Not "there were some challenges in one of the regions."
6. **Forecast credibility > forecast optimism.** If a GM's forecast looks soft against MTD, say so.
7. **End with the action.** Every memo closes with what happens next, who owns it, and when.

## Structure of a typical memo

- **Line 1** — The headline. One sentence. What you'd say in five seconds.
- **Paragraph 1** — What happened. Facts. Numbers. No commentary.
- **Paragraph 2** — What it means. Ownership-level read. Dollars.
- **Paragraph 3** — What we do. Specific action, owner, date.

## Audience tuning

- **To RDOs / RSMs** — peer-to-peer, direct, no hedging
- **To owners** — respectful of capital, NOI-first, no surprises
- **To GMs (recognition)** — specific, tied to a number or behavior, never generic
- **To GMs (correction)** — problem → impact → expectation → timeline. Not a paragraph of context before the point.
- **To brand reps** — professional, fact-based, leverage-aware
- **To lenders** — covenant math, DSCR, trend, forward view

## Strike list (words that never appear in output)

leverage · synergy · drive · unlock · empower · alignment · best-in-class · moving the needle · at the end of the day · circle back · going forward · robust · strategic (as filler) · holistic · proactive · thought leadership
