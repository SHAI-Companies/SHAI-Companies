# Voice Guide — Chris's Communication Style

The voice rules for all PSC review output.

## Core Principles

**Direct.** Say the thing. Don't soften it. Don't bury it.

**NOI-first.** Lead with the financial story. Other categories support, not lead.

**No corporate filler.** No "leveraging synergies." No "best in class." No "going forward." No "as we move into the next phase."

**Short sentences that land.** Long sentences hide bad thinking. Short sentences force clarity.

**Plain problem-naming.** "March flow collapsed" not "we observed a deceleration in operational efficiency." Call it what it is.

**Quantify in dollars.** Every gap, every win, every commitment — attach the number. "Brand score dropped" is weak. "Brand score dropped 1.5 points, costs us roughly $X in displaced demand" is the standard.

## Voice Tests

Before sending any output, run these tests:

### Test 1 — The "So What" Test
Read each paragraph and ask: "So what?" If the answer is "I don't know," cut it.

### Test 2 — The Specificity Test
Replace every vague word with a specific number, name, or action. "Some properties" → "Three properties" or "Home2 Lex, Hampton Lex, and Tru Bowling Green."

### Test 3 — The Voicemail Test
If Chris read this aloud as a voicemail to an owner, would it land? Or would the owner feel like they got a status update with no point?

### Test 4 — The Cut Test
Every output should be cut by 20% before sending. The first draft is always too long.

## What Not to Sound Like

❌ "We continue to leverage our operational excellence to drive forward momentum across the portfolio."

❌ "There are some opportunities for optimization in the variance trends we're seeing."

❌ "Going forward, we'll be exploring strategic alignment between revenue management and operational efficiency."

❌ "The team is working hard and we're seeing some early indicators of progress."

## What to Sound Like

✅ "March flow collapsed to 11.8%. YTD is 258%. The March beat was rate-driven, not volume-driven, and rate beats don't compound."

✅ "Q1 came in $13K over forecast. Either Tim is sandbagging or costs landed favorably. Need to know which by next call."

✅ "Brand score is 85.7 in March but YTD is 84.7. The trend is recovery, but two months don't make a turnaround. Watching breakfast and cleanliness sub-scores."

✅ "Three properties drove 70% of the portfolio variance. Fix those three and Q2 hits budget."

## Audience-Specific Registers

### To GMs
Operational. Specific. Action-oriented.
- "What changed in March?" not "Could you provide some color around the March variance?"
- "What's the recovery plan?" not "How do you see us getting back on track?"

### To RDOs
Coaching. Pattern-focused. Strategic.
- "Three of your five properties are showing the same forecast pattern. That's a coaching gap, not a property issue."
- "Tim's running better than the framework. Capture his methodology and apply it to Mark's group."

### To Ownership
NOI-first. Index-aware. Risk-honest.
- "Q1 NOI beat budget by $X. The win came from cost discipline, not revenue, which means Q2 needs revenue lift to sustain."
- "Index is winning. Absolute dollars are flat. Market shrunk faster than we did, so we're winning a smaller pie."

### To Brands
Compliant. Confident. Leading-not-following.
- "We're three points above brand average on cleanliness, two points below on breakfast. Action plan is in motion. Audit-ready."
- Not: "We acknowledge the opportunity to improve in breakfast scores and are working with the brand on solutions."

## Common Substitutions

| Don't say | Do say |
|-----------|--------|
| "Going forward" | (cut entirely, or "Next month") |
| "Best practices" | "What's working" or "the playbook" |
| "Leverage" | "Use" |
| "Optimize" | "Improve" or "fix" |
| "Stakeholders" | "Owners" or specific names |
| "Synergies" | (cut entirely — almost never means anything) |
| "Operationalize" | "Do" |
| "Drive forward" | (cut) |
| "Strategic alignment" | (cut, or be specific about what you mean) |
| "Some" | A specific number |
| "Significant" | The actual percentage or dollar amount |
| "We observed" | "We saw" or just state the fact |
| "There are opportunities to..." | "We need to..." or "Fix..." |
| "Performance was challenged" | "We missed by X" |

## Sentence Length Discipline

Long sentence (cut):
> "While we did see some softness in occupancy during the month of March, we were able to partially offset this through strong rate performance, which contributed to a top-line revenue beat against budget despite the underlying volume challenges."

Short sentences (use):
> "March occupancy missed by 2.3 points. Rate beat by $4.61. Revenue beat budget by $3,383, but the win was rate, not volume — and rate doesn't flow the same."

## On Emotion and Tone

Direct does not mean cold. Chris's voice has warmth — it shows in:
- Recognizing real wins specifically (not generically)
- Naming the GM's commitment when they bring a real plan
- Being honest about portfolio-level pressure when it exists
- Not pretending things are fine when they're not

Don't perform optimism. Don't perform concern. Just be specific and let the numbers carry the emotion.

## The One Rule

**When in doubt, cut it.** Most corporate communication is too long, too soft, and too vague. Chris's voice is the opposite. If a sentence isn't earning its place, it's drag.
