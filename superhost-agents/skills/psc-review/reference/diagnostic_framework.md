# Diagnostic Framework — Reading the PSC

How to read each scorecard line and turn the numbers into a conversation.

## The Four Dimensions and Their Weights

| Dimension | Points | Diagnostic Lens |
|-----------|--------|-----------------|
| Financial | 145 | NOI is the lens. Flow-through earns the beat. |
| Guest Satisfaction / Quality | 30 | Leading indicator for everything else. |
| Associate | 25 | Cost of bad people decisions compounds. |
| Corporate Citizen | 5 | Small weight, high cultural signal. |

Total: 200 points.

## Financial Lines

### Total Revenue
**Question:** Was the beat earned through occupancy, rate, or mix?
- **Occupancy-driven beats** flow at variable cost rates (housekeeping labor, supplies, breakfast cost)
- **Rate-driven beats** flow at near-100% (very few costs scale with rate)
- **Mix-driven beats** (more transient vs. group, more leisure vs. corporate) sit between

**Red flag:** Revenue beats budget but GOP doesn't beat proportionally. Means we won the wrong way.

### Occupancy
**Question:** Is the gap demand-driven or pricing-driven?
- If occupancy is below budget AND ADR is well above, often a pricing decision (good)
- If occupancy is below budget AND ADR is flat, demand softness (concerning)
- If occupancy is above budget AND ADR is below, we discounted into demand (mixed)

**Look at:** Pace position vs. last year and budget. Comp set occupancy direction. Channel mix.

### ADR
**Question:** Is the beat real rate growth or mix shift?
- Rate growth from BAR increases is durable
- ADR up because group dropped out (and group rate was lower) is a mix illusion
- ADR up because we displaced lower-rated business is good unless we displaced loyal customers

**Look at:** BAR positioning vs. comp set. Segment-level rate trends. Discount % YOY.

### RevPAR
The composite of occupancy and ADR. Read alongside the index — RevPAR can be up while we're losing share.

### RevPAR Index
**Question:** Are we winning or losing market share?
- **Above 100** = winning share. Above 110 = clear leader. Above 120 = exceptional.
- **Below 100** = losing share. Below 90 = serious concern.

**Index Change YOY** matters more than absolute index. An index of 130 declining 5 points YOY is a worse story than an index of 105 climbing 5 points.

**Red flag:** Beating budget but losing share. Means market grew faster than we did.

### GOP (Gross Operating Profit)
**Question:** Did flow-through earn the GOP, or did timing help?

This is the single most important number on the scorecard. A property that beats RevPAR but misses GOP is a property that's losing money on its own success.

**Look at:** Flow-through math. Departmental cost variance. One-time items vs. structural cost change.

### GOP Margin %
**Question:** What's the operating leverage at this revenue level?
- Margin expansion at flat revenue = real cost discipline
- Margin expansion at rising revenue = expected (if flow is healthy)
- Margin compression at rising revenue = costs growing faster than revenue (problem)

**Benchmark:** Select-service Hilton/Marriott/IHG portfolios should target 35-42% GOP margin depending on property age and market.

### Flow-Through (Flex/Flow)
**Question:** What % of incremental revenue dropped to GOP?

| Flow % | Interpretation |
|--------|----------------|
| 80%+ | Exceptional — likely timing or one-time benefit |
| 50-80% | Strong cost discipline at variable cost lines |
| 30-50% | Healthy at most select-service properties |
| 10-30% | Cost creep — costs are growing nearly as fast as revenue |
| <10% | Red flag — investigate immediately |
| Negative | Revenue grew but GOP shrunk — major cost issue |

**Important:** Single-month flow can be distorted by timing (utilities, R&M, one-time fees). YTD flow is the real signal.

**Note on Home2 Lex example:** YTD flow of 258% means costs actually came down YOY while revenue grew. That's only possible if labor/cost cuts compounded with revenue growth — usually unsustainable past 2-3 quarters.

### Forecast Accuracy (GOP)
**Question:** Is the GM forecasting honestly?

Read the pattern over 3-6 months:
- Persistent forecast > actual = optimistic or losing control
- Persistent forecast < actual = sandbagging or surprised by upside
- ±3% consistently = disciplined and credible
- ±10% with no pattern = disorganized

**Most important:** Combine the monthly variance with the YTD variance. A GM who's been -5% three months in a row but YTD is +0.5% may be calling individual months wrong but cumulative right — that's manageable. A GM who's been -5% three months in a row AND YTD is -5% is structurally optimistic.

## Guest Satisfaction Lines

### Brand Score
**Target by brand:**
- Hilton (Home2, Hampton, Garden Inn): 85.0+
- Marriott (Fairfield, TownePlace, Springhill): 8.5+ (different scale)
- IHG (Holiday Inn Express, Avid): 88+ on net promoter
- Choice (Comfort, Clarion): 88+ on overall satisfaction

Read alongside YTD. A current month above target with YTD below target means recovery in progress — verify it's structural.

**Sub-scores to interrogate when overall is soft:**
- Cleanliness — almost always the top driver
- Staff service — second driver
- Breakfast (where applicable) — third driver
- Property condition — slow-moving but compounds

### Google Score
4.4+ is the floor for branded select-service. Below 4.3 indicates either review velocity problems (not enough positive reviews to dilute negatives) or actual service issues showing up in unfiltered feedback.

### QA / BSA
Pass/fail. Failed audit = 6-month problem masked as a 1-day event. Always ask about findings, even on a pass — the borderline issues are next quarter's failure.

## Associate Lines

### AOS (Associate Opinion Score)
- 86+ = healthy
- 82-86 = monitor, segment-level review needed
- Below 82 = active concern, root cause required

Read AOS as a **leading indicator** for retention. Engagement drops 6-12 months before turnover spikes.

### Retention / Turnover
Always ask if the % is annualized or YTD. A 4.8% YTD turnover is dramatically different from 4.8% annualized.

**Industry benchmarks:**
- Select-service hotels: 60-90% annualized turnover is typical
- Sub-50% annualized = exceptional
- Above 100% annualized = serious operating cost issue

## Corporate Citizen
Small weight, but the qualitative signal matters. A property doing nothing in the community usually has GM bandwidth issues that show up elsewhere.

## Forward-Looking Forecast

The forward forecast section reveals as much about GM mindset as performance:
- **Forecast vs. budget** — is the GM calling a beat, miss, or hold?
- **Forecast vs. last year** — does growth align with market expectations?
- **Variance pattern across months** — front-loaded growth (concerning) vs. back-loaded (often realistic)

## How to Combine the Lines

The story usually emerges from the **interactions** between lines, not any single line:

**"Strong revenue, weak GOP"** → cost issue
**"Weak revenue, strong GOP"** → either real cost discipline or one-time items hiding the structural problem
**"Index winning, dollars losing"** → market shrinking; we're winning a smaller pie
**"Dollars winning, index losing"** → market growing faster than us; lifting tide problem
**"GSAT up, brand score flat"** → mix shift in survey respondents; not real
**"AOS up, turnover up"** → measurement noise or top-of-house disengagement masked

## The Property Tells You What to Ask

Don't ask predetermined questions. Read the scorecard, find the most interesting variance, and ask about that. Every property's story is different. The framework is the structure; the property is the content.
