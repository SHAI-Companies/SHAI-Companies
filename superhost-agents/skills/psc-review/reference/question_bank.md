# Question Bank — PSC Review

The full question library, organized for fast lookup during call prep.

## How to Use This

These questions are not a checklist. Pick 3-5 per call based on what the scorecard tells you. Lead with where the variance lives, not where things are clean.

**Four question types:**
- **Diagnostic** — what's actually happening underneath the number?
- **Forward-Looking** — what does next month/quarter look like and why?
- **Accountability** — what specifically is this person committing to?
- **Coaching** — what's the GM seeing or missing about their own operation?

If a GM can answer in under 10 seconds with a generic answer, you asked the wrong question. Make them think.

## Financial — Revenue & Top Line

### When Total Revenue beats budget
1. Walk me through the components — was the beat driven by occupancy, rate, or mix?
2. Is the beat repeatable or is it timing — group block, displaced demand, one-time corporate?
3. Did we leave money on the table anywhere — inventory restrictions, channel mix, length-of-stay rules?
4. If the same demand shows up next month, what would you do differently?

### When Total Revenue misses budget
1. Where did the miss come from — occupancy, rate, or both? Quantify each.
2. Was this a demand problem, a pricing problem, or a channel problem?
3. What did the comp set do — did everyone miss, or did we miss alone?
4. What's your recovery plan for next 30 days, with specific revenue targets by segment?

### Occupancy specific
1. If occupancy is below budget, did we hold rate too tight or did demand actually soften?
2. What's the pace position vs. last year and vs. budget for next 30/60/90 days?
3. Where are we losing share — weekday or weekend, transient or group, leisure or business?

### ADR specific
1. Is the ADR beat real rate growth or mix shift toward higher-rated segments?
2. What's our BAR positioning vs. comp set right now — leader, parity, or follower?
3. If ADR is up but occupancy is down, are we pricing ourselves out of demand we wanted?

## Financial — RevPAR & Index

### When RevPAR Index is winning (>100)
1. Index gain came from rate or occupancy share — which?
2. Is the gain durable, or did a comp hotel close, renovate, or have a service issue?
3. What specifically are we doing that the comp set isn't — and is it scalable to other properties?
4. If a new comp opens or our flag opens nearby, what's the share defense plan?

### When RevPAR Index is losing (<100)
1. Are we losing share on rate, occupancy, or both? What does the segmentation report say?
2. Is the comp set legitimate, or has the market shifted and we need a new comp set submission?
3. What's the comp doing that we're not — channel investment, sales activity, product condition?
4. What's the 90-day plan to close the index gap, and what specific actions move it?

### When Index Change is negative YOY
1. What changed — our performance, the comp set's performance, or the market itself?
2. If we lost share on the index, did we still grow absolute RevPAR? Which matters more here?
3. Is this a property issue or a market issue? How do you know?

## Financial — GOP, Flow-Through & Margin

### When GOP beats budget
1. Did we earn the GOP beat through flow, or did expenses time-shift into another period?
2. What's the flow-through math — and is it sustainable at next month's revenue level?
3. If revenue is flat next month, what does GOP look like? Walk me through the cost base.
4. Where is the cost discipline coming from — labor, utilities, OpEx, or all three?

### When GOP misses budget
1. Was the miss driven by revenue softness or cost overrun? Show me the bridge.
2. Which department had the largest negative variance, and what's the root cause?
3. What costs were one-time vs. structural? The structural ones are the conversation.
4. What specifically gets cut or deferred to recover next month?

### Flow-Through specific
1. Flow-through this month was X%. Why isn't it 50% — what costs grew faster than revenue?
2. If flow-through is above 100%, what's the timing or one-time benefit driving it?
3. Walk me through your variable cost levers — what flexes when revenue moves?
4. What's your flow-through target for next month, and what specifically needs to happen to hit it?

### GOP Margin specific
1. Margin moved by X points YOY. What changed — revenue mix, cost structure, or both?
2. Are we operating at peak margin for this RevPAR, or is there room?
3. What's the floor margin you'd defend if revenue dropped 10%?

## Financial — Forecast Accuracy

### When forecast was over actual (forecast too high)
1. You called X for GOP. Came in at Y. What changed between forecast and close?
2. Was the variance on the revenue side or the cost side?
3. How did your weekly forecast trend — did you see this coming and not adjust, or did it hit late?
4. What changes to your forecasting process this month so we don't repeat the variance?

### When forecast was under actual (sandbagging or surprise win)
1. Forecast was X, came in at Y. Walk me through what you didn't see.
2. Was this a real surprise or were we forecasting conservatively?
3. If you'd known what you know now, what would you have changed in real-time — pricing, cost timing, sales effort?
4. Forward forecast — is the upside continuing or was this a one-month event?

### When forecast accuracy is consistently within 3%
1. Your forecasting is tight. What's the methodology — walk me through how you build it?
2. Can we use your process as a portfolio standard? What would need to translate?

## Forward-Looking Forecast

### Next month forecast
1. Walk me through your assumptions — occupancy, ADR, segment mix, and key cost lines.
2. What's on the books vs. last year same point — pace position?
3. What's the biggest risk to your forecast, and what's the biggest upside?
4. If you miss your forecast by 10%, where does the miss come from?

### Quarter forecast
1. Quarter forecast vs. budget — are you calling a beat, miss, or hold?
2. Which months in the quarter carry the variance, and why?
3. What needs to be true in the market for your forecast to hold?
4. If Q is beating, are we pulling forward demand from Q+1?

### When forecast under-budget on a forward month
1. April forecast is X under budget. What's the demand signal — group, transient pace, comp pressure?
2. What sales or revenue actions are you taking in the next 14 days to close the gap?
3. If we don't close the gap, what cost actions offset on the GOP line?

### When forecast over-budget on a forward month
1. Forecast is X over budget. What's giving you confidence — booked business, market read, or hope?
2. What gets pulled forward from the next month to make it happen?
3. If the upside materializes, what's the plan to capture it operationally — labor, supplies, service standards?

## Guest Satisfaction & Quality

### When Brand Score is above target
1. What specifically is driving the score — service moments, product condition, or expectation management?
2. What's your top GSAT comment trend over the last 60 days — positive and negative?
3. If the brand score dropped 2 points next month, where would the leak come from first?
4. How are you sustaining this — is it dependent on specific people or is it a system?

### When Brand Score is below target
1. What's the root cause — service execution, product issue, or expectation gap?
2. Show me the verbatim comment trend — what are guests actually saying?
3. Which department drives the lowest sub-scores, and what's the action plan there?
4. What's your 60-day plan to recover, and what's the leading indicator we're watching weekly?

### Google Score
1. If Google score is dropping, what's the recent review trend — and are we responding to every one?
2. What's our review velocity vs. the comp set — are we generating enough reviews to dilute the negatives?
3. Are we soliciting reviews systematically at checkout, or hoping?

### QA / BSA
1. Walk me through your QA prep cadence — what's checked weekly vs. monthly?
2. What's the one finding you're most worried about, and what's the fix path?
3. If we got an unannounced audit tomorrow, where would we fail?

## Associate — AOS, Retention & Team Health

### When AOS is at or above target
1. What's driving the engagement — leadership, recognition, growth, or culture? Be specific.
2. Which department has the highest engagement, and what are they doing differently?
3. If your top three performers left tomorrow, what breaks?
4. What's the one engagement risk you're watching that the AOS doesn't capture?

### When AOS is below target
1. Where's the disengagement concentrated — department, shift, or tenure?
2. What did the verbatim comments tell you, and which themes are you acting on?
3. What's your action plan with timeline, and how will we know it's working?
4. Is this a leadership issue, a workload issue, or a compensation issue?

### Retention / Turnover
1. Turnover at X% — is that calculated annualized or YTD? Compare it to portfolio average.
2. Where is the turnover concentrated — entry-level, supervisor, or department head?
3. What's the cost of turnover this month — recruiting, training, OT coverage?
4. Which positions are hardest to fill right now, and what's the bench plan?

### Key positions
1. Where do you have key-position risk in the next six months — GM, AGM, DOS, Chief Engineer?
2. Who on your team is ready for promotion, and what's the development plan?
3. If you got hit by a bus, who runs this hotel tomorrow?

## Questions by Score Tier

### Excellent (≥150) — Capture & Replicate
1. What's the one thing driving this score that didn't exist 12 months ago?
2. If we replicated your playbook at three other properties, what would translate and what wouldn't?
3. What's your biggest risk — complacency, comp set response, or losing key people?
4. Where would you invest if I gave you $50K of discretionary capital?

### On Track (120-149) — Verify Durability
1. Which scorecard category is closest to slipping into Watch territory?
2. What's the leading indicator that tells you this score holds next month?
3. Where's the over-performance masking under-performance somewhere else?
4. What needs to break right for you to push into Excellent?

### Watch (90-119) — Diagnose & Direct
1. Which two scorecard lines are dragging the score, and what's the dollar impact of fixing each?
2. What's the 60-day plan to move score back above 120, with weekly checkpoints?
3. What support do you need from me, the RDO, or corporate to move the needle?
4. If the score doesn't recover in 60 days, what's the next escalation step?

### At Risk (60-89) — Intervene
1. What's the single biggest issue, and is it a people issue, a market issue, or a leadership issue?
2. What's your written 30-day reset plan — actions, owners, dates, expected impact?
3. Why isn't this conversation happening weekly already?
4. What's the trigger for moving this to formal performance management?

### Needs Action (<60) — Crisis Mode
1. What happens this week — not this month — to stabilize?
2. Are we confident in the leadership team to execute the recovery, or is that the problem?
3. What capital or operational support do we need to deploy immediately?
4. What's the timeline before we're forced into a structural change?

## Questions by Forecast Pattern

### Pattern: Forecast > Actual repeatedly (Optimistic)
1. Three months in a row your forecast missed high. What's the disconnect between your model and reality?
2. Are you forecasting based on hope, last year, or pace data?
3. What's changed in your weekly forecast review process to catch this earlier?
4. What number can you defend with conviction for next month?

### Pattern: Forecast < Actual repeatedly (Sandbagging)
1. You've beaten forecast three months running. Why are we still calling conservative numbers?
2. If forecast is the commitment, the commitment is too low. What's the real number?
3. What's the upside next month, called honestly?

### Pattern: High variance, no direction (Disorganized)
1. Your forecast variance has been ±10% for four months. Where's the model breaking?
2. Walk me through your forecast build — segment by segment, cost line by cost line.
3. Do you need RDO or revenue management support to rebuild the methodology?

## Closing the Call — Action Commitment

End every call with these. No exceptions.

1. What are the three things you're committing to do in the next 30 days?
2. Who owns each one, and what's the date?
3. What's the dollar impact if we execute, and the dollar exposure if we don't?
4. What do you need from me — capital, alignment, support, or a decision?
5. What will be different on this scorecard 30 days from now, and how will I know?

## The 5 Questions That Matter Most

If a call gets cut short, these five carry the whole conversation:

| # | Question | What It Reveals |
|---|----------|-----------------|
| 1 | Walk me through the variance on the largest miss. | Whether the GM understands their own P&L. |
| 2 | Was the GOP beat earned by flow, or by timing? | Whether the win is real or temporary. |
| 3 | What's your forecast for next month, and what assumption is the most fragile? | Forecast credibility and risk awareness. |
| 4 | If revenue drops 10% next month, what's the cost response? | Whether there's a real plan or just optimism. |
| 5 | What are the three things you're committing to in the next 30 days? | Action exit. Owner, date, dollar impact. |

## Traps to Avoid

These feel productive but produce nothing:
- "How are things going?"
- "What's working well?"
- "Any concerns?"
- "What can I do to help?"
- "Why didn't we hit budget?"

Replace with:
- "Walk me through the variance on line X."
- "What specifically changed between forecast and actual?"
- "What's the dollar impact of the biggest gap, and what's the fix path?"
- "What are you committing to in the next 30 days?"
- "What needs to be true for this number to improve next month?"
